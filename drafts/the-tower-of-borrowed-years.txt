Marek Vale is a third-grade clerk in the Chamber of Accounts at Bell Stair. The Tower lends what people cannot wait for — a year of youth, a year of a trade, a season's recovery — and takes the pledge out of the future. Every loan is written in five parts: need, pledge, scope, witness, settlement. A loan missing one is not void. It is false-witnessed, and the Tower goes looking for a person to carry it. Marek's job is to read the receipt against the sealed terms and sign it, or find the clause that stops it. Then a Tallow Ward water assessment reaches his bench with a false recital, eleven unsigned sureties, and a settlement that must be sealed before the twelfth or there is no gate and the low kitchens go under. Marek finds a remedy in the margin of the Tower's founding page: a standing hold, held by the clerk of record, an office vacant since the third century. It moves the settlement to the end of the year. It does not cancel the debt. It puts the delay in the clerk's own yearline. He holds it for six bells. At the sixth, the collection walks the chain to the living signature at the foot of the entry: Ondra Kelb, twenty, three weeks in the post, whose hand goes still. By morning there is a new mark on Marek's forearm, a ruled line with four notches, not a wound. Suspended and unpaid, Marek keeps reading. The paper leads him out of Tallow to Rainmarket, where a seed cooperative's loan favours the sowing season and makes the whole low ground bear its failure. It leads him to the Sable Compact, a claims house that buys and bundles paper until the last name on the chain is a district. It leads him back to Hesper Rann, the supervisor whose lie kept Tallow above water. His brother Tomas is working a vigor loan their mother witnessed, and his hands are going. The Trust wants a villain who fits in a box. The Compact wants the blank line in the Tower's oldest ledger, a claim with nobody behind it. Marek wants a room where a person can ask what they are standing in before the collection comes. He can read a page by touch, which is how he finds the clause nobody else can see. The reading costs him a headache, a taste of iron, a hand that shakes. The holds cost more. Every delay has to be carried somewhere, and the Tower's answer is always the same: put the cost on a district, a ward, a class, and find a person to sign. Marek is one clerk with a notched arm and a vacant office. He can hold a settlement for six bells. The Tower has an answer for the seventh.
